Delivery workers face specific procedures because they are micro-entrepreneurs (self-employed). It is also hard to understand letters from the authorities and to know which steps to take. The Houses offer help, with regular hours or by appointment.

## What you can find in a House

- **Help with everyday procedures**: URSSAF (social contributions) and tax declarations, requests to pay a fine in instalments, CAF (family allowance) declarations, writing letters to the authorities.
- **Access to rights**: many delivery workers do not know the standard schemes they can use, such as the Aide médicale d'État (AME, state medical aid), the Complémentaire santé solidaire (supplementary health cover), the Droit au logement opposable (enforceable right to housing) or reduced transport fares.
- **Follow-up of residence permit applications**, with advice from lawyers who specialise in immigration law.
- **Public writer**: help to write letters, to family or to the authorities.
- **Free-access computers** with a printer, to do your procedures yourself.

## Partner organisations

- **Point Justice**: general or specialised legal advice sessions (labour law, social protection).
- **Bus des avocats**: open sessions for everyone, and sessions specialised in immigration law and asylum law.
- **La Cimade**: welcome and support for foreign people with their procedures (residence permit, naturalisation, family reunification).
- **ACINA**: social and professional support for newcomers.
- **Watizat**: addresses and useful information for exiled people, social rights, asylum and residence permits.
- **PIMMS**: free individual help, without appointment, to understand your letters and procedures.
- **DAL (Droit au logement)**: rehousing of families excluded from housing.
- **Droits d'urgence**: legal advice sessions and case follow-up with lawyers.

The organisations present vary by city. Ask the nearest House.
